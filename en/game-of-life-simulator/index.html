Conway's Game of Life Simulator with Pattern Detection

Runs Conway's Game of Life on a grid you type, on a torus or with dead edges, and reports the population, snapshots and cycles.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Life has two rules and endless behavior: a live cell with two or three live neighbours survives, and a dead cell with exactly three becomes live. Draw a glider with # and . characters and watch it walk across the grid.

The tool prints snapshots and the population per generation and detects when the grid repeats, telling you whether it is a still life or an oscillator and its period, or that everything died. Results were compared with an independent implementation on random grids with both edge rules. It is text-based, not a clickable canvas.

How to use it

  1. Type the grid with # for live and . for dead cells.
  2. Set the number of generations.
  3. Choose wrap-around (torus) or bounded edges.
  4. Read the snapshots, population list and the cycle note.

Frequently asked questions

Which cell characters work?

#, O, o, * and 1 are live; everything else is dead.

What is the largest grid?

160 columns by 120 rows.

Why does a glider come back?

On a torus it wraps around the edges.

What about other rules?

For one-dimensional rules like Rule 30 use the elementary cellular automaton tool.
