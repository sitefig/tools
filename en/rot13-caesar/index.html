ROT13 / Caesar Cipher

Apply ROT13 or a Caesar shift of any size to your text, or switch to ROT47 to cover punctuation and digits as well. Use a negative shift to decode.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

ROT13 is the old internet way of hiding spoilers, puzzle answers and punchlines without encrypting anything. The Caesar cipher is the same idea with any shift, and it is a favourite in beginner puzzles, geocaching clues and cryptography lessons.

This tool shifts only the letters, keeps case and leaves digits and punctuation alone. ROT13 is its own inverse, so running it twice returns the original, while any other shift is undone by the negative value. ROT47 extends the idea to all printable ASCII characters. It is a toy cipher that any person can break, so do not use it to protect anything.

How to use it

  1. Paste your text into the Text box.
  2. Leave the shift at 13 for ROT13, or enter another number. Use a negative number to decode.
  3. Tick "ROT47 instead" if you want to rotate every printable ASCII character.
  4. Copy the result.

Frequently asked questions

Is ROT13 secure?

No. It has only one possible key, so anyone can reverse it instantly. Use it for hiding spoilers and puzzles, never for secrets. For real protection, use proper encryption such as the AES tools on this site.

How do I decode a Caesar cipher?

Enter the negative of the shift that was used. If the text was shifted by 5, use -5. If you do not know the shift, try values from 1 to 25 until readable words appear.

What is the difference between ROT13 and ROT47?

ROT13 rotates only the 26 letters. ROT47 rotates the 94 printable ASCII characters from ! to ~, so digits and punctuation change too. Like ROT13, applying ROT47 twice gives back the original.

What happens to accented letters?

Only the unaccented letters A to Z and a to z are shifted. Accented letters, other alphabets, digits and punctuation pass through unchanged in Caesar mode.
