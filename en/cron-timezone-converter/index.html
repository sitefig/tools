Cron Time Zone Converter

Enter a five-field cron expression written for one time zone and get the equivalent for another, with the hour and, when the shift crosses midnight, the day fields adjusted.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Many schedulers only understand UTC. A job that must run at 09:00 in Brussels then needs a different expression in summer and winter, and a late-evening job in New York lands on the next day in UTC, so the day-of-week field has to move too. Doing this by hand is where weekday jobs end up running on a Saturday.

This converter calculates the offset between the two zones on a reference date, shifts the hours and rolls the day fields forward or back as needed. It also warns about the daylight saving catch. A shifted expression is only right while both zones keep that offset. The work happens in your browser and your schedule is not uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Paste the cron expression with five fields: minute, hour, day of month, month and day of week.
  2. Enter the time zone it is written for and the time zone you want, as IANA names.
  3. Optionally set a reference date so the correct daylight saving offset is used.
  4. Copy the converted expression and read the notes about day shifts and clock changes.

Frequently asked questions

Will the converted job still be right after daylight saving changes?

Not necessarily. The result is exact only while both zones keep the offset of your reference date. For year-round local time, set the time zone in the scheduler itself if it supports one.

What cron syntax does it support?

Standard five-field cron with lists, ranges, steps and day names. Complex expressions, such as a step on a half-hour offset, may need manual conversion, and the tool will say so.

Why did my day-of-week field change?

If the shift moves the time past midnight, the job runs on a different calendar day in the target zone. The tool moves each listed day forward or back by one so the job still fires on the intended day.
