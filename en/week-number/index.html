Week Number Calculator

Pick a date. The tool shows its ISO 8601 week number, the week-based year and the weekday.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Planning calendars, payroll schedules, shipping notices and sprint boards often refer to week 37 rather than a date. The trouble is that week numbering is not universal: in some countries weeks start on Sunday and week 1 is the one containing 1 January. The ISO 8601 system, common across Europe and in business software, starts on Monday and makes week 1 the week with the first Thursday. As a result, the last days of December can belong to week 1 of the next year, and early January can sit in week 52 or 53 of the previous one. This calculator applies the ISO rule and shows the week-year, so edge cases are not a surprise. The date stays in your browser.

How to use it

  1. Pick a date.
  2. Read the ISO week number and the week-based year.
  3. Check the weekday shown beside the date if you need to place it in the week.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ISO 8601 week rule?

Weeks run Monday to Sunday, and week 1 is the week that contains the year's first Thursday, which is the same as the week containing 4 January.

Why can 30 December be in week 1?

Because the week belongs to the year that holds its Thursday. If Thursday falls on 2 January, the Monday before it, in late December, is part of week 1 of the new year.

Is this the same as the week number in Excel or the US?

Not always. US calendars and some Excel settings start weeks on Sunday and count the week with 1 January as week 1, so numbers can differ by one.

Can a year have 53 weeks?

Yes. Some years have 53 ISO weeks, typically when 1 January falls on a Thursday, or on a Wednesday in a leap year.
