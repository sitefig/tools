robots.txt Pattern to Regex Converter (for Log Analysis)

Turns robots.txt path patterns into regular expressions anchored at the start of the path.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

To count how often bots hit blocked areas in your logs, you need the same patterns as regular expressions. Converting by hand means escaping dots and question marks and mapping * to .* correctly.

Paste your rules and copy the regexes. The tool does not apply the longest-match logic between Allow and Disallow; that stays up to you or to the compliance log checker.

How to use it

  1. Paste rules with or without the Allow or Disallow prefix.
  2. Copy the regex for each pattern.
  3. Use it with grep -E, awk or a log platform.
  4. Apply it to the path plus query string.

Frequently asked questions

Is the regex case sensitive?

Yes, like robots.txt.

Why anchor with ^?

Patterns match from the start of the path.

Does it handle $?

Yes, it becomes an end-of-string anchor.

Can it resolve conflicts?

No. The compliance log checker applies the full logic.
