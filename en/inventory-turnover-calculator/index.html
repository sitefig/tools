Inventory Turnover and Days of Inventory Calculator

Calculates how many times stock turns over in a period and how many days it sits.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Turnover shows how fast stock sells. Too low means cash sits on shelves, and too high can mean lost sales from stock-outs. Dividing by 365 gives days of inventory, which is easier to read.

Use cost of goods sold, not revenue, and inventory valued at cost so both sides share a basis. What counts as a good value differs a lot between products, so compare with your own past figures and similar businesses.

How to use it

  1. Enter cost of goods sold for the period.
  2. Enter inventory value at the start and end.
  3. Set days in the period and read turnover and days.

Frequently asked questions

Why use average inventory?

Stock changes during the period, so averaging start and end smooths it.

Can I use revenue instead of COGS?

Not for the standard ratio. It mixes cost and price bases.

What is a good turnover?

It depends on the product. Compare with your history and peers.
