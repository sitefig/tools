Inflation Calculator

Enter an amount, an annual inflation rate and a number of years. The tool shows the future cost, the equivalent value today and the purchasing power lost.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Prices drift upwards, and a sum that looks comfortable now buys less every year. That matters when you plan a pension, set a savings target, agree a multi-year contract or ask for a pay rise. The calculation is simple compounding, but few people do it in their heads for a ten or twenty year span. This tool shows both directions: what something that costs 1,000 now might cost later, and what 1,000 received later is worth in current prices. It also states the share of purchasing power lost. It assumes a single constant rate, which real inflation is not, so treat it as a scenario tool. It runs locally in your browser.

How to use it

  1. Enter the amount today.
  2. Enter the expected inflation rate per year and the number of years.
  3. Read the future cost, the value in current terms and the purchasing power lost.

Frequently asked questions

What formula does the calculator use?

Future cost equals the amount times (1 plus the rate) to the power of the years. Dividing by the same factor gives what a future amount is worth in current prices.

Does it use real historical inflation data?

No. It applies the constant rate you type and does not fetch consumer price indices. For past periods, use the official inflation figures from your national statistics office.

Why does purchasing power fall by less than the rate times years?

Because the effect compounds. At 3% for 10 years, prices rise about 34%, but purchasing power falls by about 25.6%, since the loss is measured against the higher future price.

Can I enter a negative rate for deflation?

Yes, a negative number is accepted. The future cost then falls and purchasing power rises. The years value can also be fractional.
