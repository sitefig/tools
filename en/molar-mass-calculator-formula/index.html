Molar Mass Calculator from a Chemical Formula (IUPAC Atomic Weights)

Reads a chemical formula, including brackets and hydrates, and gives the molar mass with each element contribution, percent by mass and the IUPAC uncertainty.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Stoichiometry starts with molar mass. Type Ca(OH)2 or CuSO4·5H2O and the tool adds up the atoms, including nested brackets and the water of crystallization, and shows how much of the mass each element carries.

The atomic weights are the abridged standard atomic weights 2024 from the IUPAC Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW), copied from its website with their uncertainties. They are rounded as published, so a sum can differ in the last digit from tools with longer tables. Elements with no stable isotopes have no standard atomic weight and are refused rather than guessed. The results were compared with the python periodictable package.

How to use it

  1. Type one formula per line, for example C6H12O6.
  2. Use brackets for groups and a dot or middle dot for hydrates.
  3. Read the table of elements and the molar mass.
  4. Check the source line if you need to cite the weights.

Frequently asked questions

Why does case matter?

Co is cobalt but CO is carbon monoxide, so symbols must be written correctly.

Why is technetium refused?

It has no standard atomic weight because it has no stable isotope.

Are charges supported?

No. Write the neutral formula.

How exact is the uncertainty?

It is the plain sum of the published plus-minus values, a worst case bound.
