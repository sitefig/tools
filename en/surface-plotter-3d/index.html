3D Surface Plotter z = f(x, y)

Draws a surface z = f(x, y) as a depth-sorted, height-colored mesh. You turn it by changing two angles.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Two-variable functions are hard to picture from the formula alone. A quick surface plot shows peaks, saddles and ripples, such as sin(x) * cos(y) or x^2 - y^2.

The plot is a static image made from filled polygons sorted by depth, with color showing height from blue to red. Because it is not a mouse-driven 3D scene, you rotate it by editing the azimuth and elevation numbers, which also keeps it usable with a keyboard. Very steep surfaces can show small sorting artefacts.

How to use it

  1. Type the formula in x and y, for example sin(sqrt(x^2 + y^2)).
  2. Set the x and y ranges and the grid size.
  3. Change azimuth and elevation to look from another side.
  4. Check the z range printed below the picture.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rotate with the mouse?

No. Use the angle fields; this keeps the tool accessible and dependency-free.

Why are parts missing?

Points where the function is undefined (such as a division by zero) are left out.

How many points does it use?

Up to 60 by 60 cells, so 3600 polygons.

Can I save the picture?

Use your browser to save or print the page, or take a screenshot.
