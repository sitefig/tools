Sun Times Table with Twilight and Polar Day for Date Ranges

Computes sun times for a place and date range from the NOAA solar equations, shown in the time zone you choose.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Enter a location and a date and you get dawn, sunrise, solar noon, sunset and dusk for civil, nautical and astronomical twilight. The equations are those of the NOAA solar calculator (after Meeus). Results were compared with PyEphem for seven place and date cases and agree within seconds in most cases and under one minute in all.

Above the polar circles the sun can stay up or down all day; the tool says so instead of showing nonsense. Times use the daylight saving rules of the IANA time zone you type. Terrain, buildings and weather are ignored.

How to use it

  1. Enter the location as lat, lon.
  2. Set a start date and the number of days.
  3. Type an IANA time zone such as Europe/London.
  4. Read or copy the table.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate?

About a minute at mid latitudes; worse near the poles.

Which sun position counts as sunrise?

The upper limb with standard refraction, a zenith of 90.833 degrees.

Why does a time show (20/12)?

The event falls on another local date than the row.

Can I use a UTC offset?

Use an IANA name; UTC works for none.
