Gamepad Tester

Connect a game controller, press a button, and see live values for every button and axis, plus a button to test vibration.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Controllers fail in small ways: a stick that drifts, a trigger that does not reach full travel, a button that registers twice or not at all. Before returning a pad, blaming a game or filing a bug, it helps to see what the hardware actually reports.

This tester reads the browser's Gamepad API and lists each connected controller with its mapping, a numbered row of buttons that highlight with their pressure when pressed, and every axis with a numeric value and a bar. Resting sticks should sit near zero, and a steady non-zero value points to drift. A vibration button sends a short rumble to controllers that support it. Browsers only reveal a controller after you press a button on it. Nothing leaves your device.

How to use it

  1. Connect your controller by USB or Bluetooth.
  2. Press any button so the browser detects it.
  3. Press buttons and move sticks to see values change.
  4. Press Test vibration to check the rumble motors.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it say to connect a controller?

Browsers hide gamepads until you press a button on one, for privacy. Connect it, then press any button while the page is focused. If nothing appears, try another USB port or reconnect Bluetooth.

How do I spot stick drift?

Leave the sticks untouched and read the axis values. They should be within a few thousandths of zero. A steady value like 0.1 or higher indicates drift, though games often apply a dead zone to hide small amounts.

What does the mapping say?

A standard mapping means the browser arranged the buttons in the usual layout. If the mapping reads none, button numbers are device-specific, so they may not match the layout of other controllers.

Does vibration work on all controllers?

No. It needs browser support and a controller that exposes a vibration actuator. Many controllers on some systems do not, so a missing rumble does not always mean the motors are faulty.
