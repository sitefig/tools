N-gram Extractor: Most Frequent 2, 3 and 4-Word Phrases

Counts repeated phrases of two to eight words in your text, with a choice of how stop words are treated. Useful for spotting key phrases and over-repetition.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Single-word counts miss what a page is about. Phrases such as "trail running shoes" carry the topic. Counting two to four-word phrases shows which ones a text repeats, and where the wording gets repetitive.

Phrases do not cross sentence ends, commas or brackets. By default, phrases that start or end with a stop word such as "the" or "of" are dropped. There is no ideal count: use it to spot repetition and to compare with competitor text.

How to use it

  1. Paste your text.
  2. Set the shortest and longest phrase length.
  3. Choose how stop words are handled and the minimum count.
  4. Read the tables, one per length.

Frequently asked questions

How does this differ from keyword density?

The keyword density tool covers phrases up to three words. This one goes up to eight and shows counts per 1,000 words.

Why are some phrases missing?

They appear less often than the minimum count or contain a stop word under the current setting.

Does it work for other languages?

The stop word list is English. Other languages work with stop words set to keep all.

Is there a safe count?

No. Natural writing repeats the main phrase. Rewrite only when it reads badly.
