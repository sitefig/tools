Glassmorphism Generator

Produces the CSS for a frosted-glass card: a translucent background, backdrop-filter blur and saturation, a faint border and rounded corners. A preview shows it over a colourful backdrop.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Glassmorphism, the frosted-glass look, depends on a handful of properties working together: a semi-transparent fill, a backdrop blur, a lighter border to catch the edge and a background behind it with enough colour to show off the blur. Getting the balance wrong gives either a muddy grey box or a card that is unreadable.

Pick the glass colour, transparency, blur amount, saturation, border strength and radius, and compare the card against a gradient backdrop. The output includes the prefixed backdrop-filter that older WebKit browsers need. Remember that blurred backgrounds can hurt text contrast and are costly on weak devices. Everything runs locally in your browser.

How to use it

  1. Choose the glass colour and the transparency percentage.
  2. Adjust blur, saturation, border opacity and radius.
  3. Check the preview card against the gradient backdrop.
  4. Copy the CSS block into your stylesheet.

Frequently asked questions

Does backdrop-filter work everywhere?

It is supported in current browsers, and the output includes the -webkit- prefix for older Safari. Where it is unsupported the card falls back to the translucent colour without blur, so set a fallback that stays readable.

How do I keep text readable on glass?

Use a stronger fill, a darker or lighter text colour with enough contrast against the average backdrop, and avoid placing glass over very busy areas. Test the text colour with a contrast checker.

Why does my card look flat?

Blur is only visible if there is something colourful or detailed behind the card. On a plain white page you will see almost nothing. Place it over an image or gradient.

What does the saturation setting do?

It boosts or reduces the colour intensity of whatever is behind the glass. A value above 100% makes the blurred colours richer, which helps the frosted effect stand out.
