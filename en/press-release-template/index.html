Press Release Template

Fill in the company, headline, city, date, announcement, quote and contact details to get a press release in the standard structure, ready to edit.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Journalists receive many releases a day and skim for the same elements: a clear headline, a dateline, the news in the first paragraph, a quote, the background and a contact. Writing from a blank page often buries the news under company history.

This template arranges your answers in the conventional order: FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE, headline, city and date, what is being announced and why it matters, a quote, a link, the "About" paragraph, the media contact and the closing ###. It does not write the news for you, so keep the facts concrete and the language plain. Your text is assembled in the browser and not uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Fill in the company name, headline, city and date.
  2. Write the announcement in one or two sentences, then add why it matters.
  3. Add a quote with the speaker's name and title, a link, a press contact and the about text.
  4. Copy the finished text and edit it into your own voice.

Frequently asked questions

What should the first paragraph contain?

The news itself: who, what, when, where and why it matters, in one or two sentences. Put background after that. Many readers, and some journalists, will not get past the opening.

How long should a press release be?

Usually one page, around 300 to 500 words. The template produces a short skeleton, so expand the body only with facts the reader needs.

Does it check facts or style?

No. It only arranges what you type. Check names, dates and claims yourself, and avoid hype words that a journalist will cut anyway.

Is the content saved or sent anywhere?

No. The release is built in your browser and disappears when you close the page. Copy it into your own document if you want to keep it.
