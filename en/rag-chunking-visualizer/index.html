RAG Chunking Visualizer

Paste a document and see it cut into colour-coded chunks by fixed window, sentence or recursive splitting, with the overlapping text highlighted.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Chunking is one of the most influential settings in a retrieval pipeline, and it is hard to judge from numbers alone. Chunks that split a sentence, a table or a list in the middle give poor search results, and too much overlap just wastes storage.

This visualiser shows the actual text of each chunk in its own colour block, with a header giving the range and size, and highlights the part repeated from the previous chunk. Strategies: a fixed window, sentence groups, or recursive splitting that tries paragraphs, then lines, then sentences, then words. With sentence and recursive modes the overlap works in whole segments, so it can be zero when segments are large. Sizes can be in characters, words or approximate tokens.

How to use it

  1. Paste the document text.
  2. Choose chunk size, overlap, the unit and a strategy.
  3. Read the coloured chunks and the statistics, then adjust until the boundaries look right.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my overlap zero?

In sentence and recursive modes overlap is made of whole segments. If each segment is larger than the overlap you asked for, nothing fits and there is no overlap. Use fixed window for exact overlap.

How does recursive splitting work?

It splits on paragraph breaks first, then single line breaks, sentence ends and finally spaces, and only goes to the next level when a piece is still larger than the chunk size.

What does the token unit mean?

A rough conversion of four characters per token. It is not a real tokenizer, so counts are approximate and may differ from your embedding model.

Can I export the chunks?

The view is for inspection. Use the Text Chunk Splitter if you want plain numbered chunks that you can copy.

Tools often used together

  • Text Chunk Splitter (RAG)Split text into overlapping chunks by characters or approximate tokens, preferring paragraph/sentence boundaries.
  • LLM Token Count Estimator (approximate)ESTIMATE of token counts for GPT-4o, GPT-4, Claude, DeepSeek and Llama from text, using per-model chars-per-token ratios and a word/punctuation heuristic. No vocabulary is available offline, so counts are approximate (typically within 10-20% for English).
  • Embedding Vector SimilarityCompare embedding vectors you paste (JSON arrays or numbers, one vector per line): cosine similarity, dot product, Euclidean and Manhattan distance, plus a similarity matrix and best matches for several vectors. It does not create embeddings.
  • LLM Context Window ShrinkerShrink text to fit a token budget: keep head, tail, both ends, drop duplicate lines, or keep the most informative sentences in original order (frequency-based extractive summary). Token counts are estimates (characters / ratio), not a real tokenizer.
  • Truncate / Chunk Text by Tokens (tiktoken)Cut text to at most N tokens, or split it into chunks of at most N tokens with overlap, always on a token boundary that does not split a UTF-8 character. Exact cl100k_base / o200k_base. Only OpenAI encodings (cl100k_base, o200k_base) are included. Claude, DeepSeek and Gemini tokenizers are NOT included (their vocabularies are not available offline): use the llm-token-estimator tool for approximate counts of those.
  • Embedding Cost CalculatorEstimate the cost of embedding a corpus: documents x average tokens (or characters / words), re-embedding runs, and vector storage size (dimensions x 4 bytes, plus HNSW overhead estimate). Prices are list prices per 1M tokens at the time of writing and change often: override with "Custom price" and check your provider. Token counts from characters are an approximation (about 4 characters per English token).
