Email Address Validator (Syntax, Typos, Role)

Check a list of email addresses for syntax errors, over-long parts, bad domains and likely typos like gmial.com.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

The validator checks the local part (allowed characters, dots, 64-character limit), the domain (labels, hyphens, 63-character labels, TLD), total length (254), internationalised domains and the quoted-string form. Warnings flag role addresses such as admin@ or info@, plus-tag addresses and domains within two edits of popular providers.

Syntax validity does not mean a mailbox exists. Confirming that needs a DNS lookup and an SMTP conversation or, better, a confirmation email, which a browser page cannot do. It also does not check for disposable-domain lists.

No data is sent anywhere: the work happens in your browser, so you can use it offline once the page has loaded.

How to use it

  1. Paste addresses one per line.
  2. Run the validator.
  3. Fix entries marked FAIL; look at the typo hints.
  4. Send a confirmation email to prove ownership.

Frequently asked questions

Can it tell if the address exists?

No. Only a message to the address (or a trusted verification service) can.

Why accept plus addresses?

They are valid and widely used, so they only get a note.

Are non-ASCII addresses valid?

Yes under SMTPUTF8, though not every system accepts them; the tool warns.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Everything is processed in your browser, and the page makes no network requests with your input.
