Headline Analyzer

Paste one or more headlines and get a score out of 100 based on length, numbers, power words, tone and structure, plus a note on whether the headline may truncate in search results.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

You often write ten possible titles for one article, and a decision based on gut feeling alone is shaky. A quick comparison on length, concrete numbers and strong words is a useful second opinion, especially when a title will be cut off in search results after around 60 characters.

This analyser applies a simple scoring recipe to each line: a sweet spot for length, a bonus for digits, a list of power words, a nudge for positive or negative tone, and a starter such as how or why. It prints the score, character count, word count and the power words found. The score is a heuristic and not a ranking or click-through prediction.

How to use it

  1. Paste your headlines, one per line.
  2. Run the analyser.
  3. Compare the scores, lengths and power words for each headline.
  4. Shorten any headline flagged as possibly truncated, then rewrite and test again.

Frequently asked questions

What does the score measure?

It adds points for a length of about 40 to 65 characters, a number in the title, up to three power words, some emotional tone, a how or why opening and a moderate word count. It is a writing aid, not an SEO metric.

Will a high score make my page rank?

No. Rankings depend on content, links and many other factors. A better headline can help click-through, but the score has no connection to any search engine.

Does it work for languages other than English?

Not well. The power word and tone lists are English, so only length and numbers are meaningful in other languages.

How can I check how the title looks in Google?

Use the SERP Snippet or Title Length Checker. They show a preview and measure the title against typical display limits.
