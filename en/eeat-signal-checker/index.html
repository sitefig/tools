E-E-A-T & Freshness Signal Checker

Check whether a page shows the visible signals of experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust, and how fresh its dates are.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

The checker looks for a named author or reviewer, stated credentials or experience, links to About, Contact, Privacy and editorial policy pages, external sources, organization or publisher data and sameAs profiles in JSON-LD, review markup, and machine-readable publish or update dates. It computes the age of the newest date and flags content older than your threshold.

E-E-A-T is a quality concept used by Google's human raters, not a switch you can turn on. This tool only reports what is present in the markup. Add signals only where they are true: invented credentials or reviews hurt users and break guidelines.

No data is sent anywhere: the work happens in your browser, so you can use it offline once the page has loaded.

How to use it

  1. Paste the page HTML.
  2. Set how many months count as stale and, if needed, the date to check against.
  3. Run it and note which signals are missing.
  4. Add genuine author, source and update information where it belongs.

Frequently asked questions

Is E-E-A-T a ranking factor?

Google describes it as a framework for evaluating quality, not a single factor or score.

Why check dates?

Outdated pages can mislead. Showing when content was updated helps readers and is picked up in structured data.

Does it verify credentials?

No. It only detects that credentials are mentioned.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Everything is processed in your browser, and the page makes no network requests with your input.
