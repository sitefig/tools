Audio Equalizer (7-Band, Export WAV)

Boosts or cuts seven frequency bands of an audio file and saves the processed result as WAV. Presets cover common jobs, or set each gain yourself.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

A muddy voice recording, a thin laptop speaker mix or a harsh cymbal can often be fixed with a few decibels at the right frequency. A graphic equalizer gives you those handles without a full editor.

The two outer bands are shelf filters, and the five inner bands are peaking filters about one octave wide, computed with the standard RBJ biquad formulas. Neighbouring bands overlap, so a single band never acts alone. Raising several bands adds loudness and can clip, so the tool can lower the output gain automatically.

How to use it

  1. Choose an audio file.
  2. Pick a preset, or choose "custom" and enter gains for each band.
  3. Keep "lower the gain if the result would clip" ticked.
  4. Press Run, listen, and download the WAV.

Frequently asked questions

How much gain is safe?

Stay within about 6 dB per band. Large boosts amplify noise and can distort.

Why does the 100 Hz shelf show only half the gain at 100 Hz?

A shelf reaches its full gain about an octave past its corner frequency. At the corner it is at half the gain in decibels.

Can I cut mains hum?

Not precisely. The bands are one octave wide; a notch filter would be narrower.

Is it the same as a live equalizer?

No. This processes the file and exports it. It does not play through an equalizer in real time.
