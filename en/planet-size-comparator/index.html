Planet and Moon Size Comparator (True Relative Scale)

Draws the bodies you pick side by side at true relative size and lists their diameters, size ratios, volume ratios and masses.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Pictures in textbooks rarely show how much bigger Jupiter is than Earth. Pick Earth, Jupiter, the Moon and Mars and the circles are drawn to the same scale, with a table of ratios: Jupiter is about 11 Earth diameters across and roughly 1400 Earth volumes.

The numbers are copied from the NASA Goddard Planetary Fact Sheet; the Sun diameter is twice its volumetric mean radius. Distances are not to scale. When you add the Sun, small bodies shrink to a dot, which is the point of the comparison.

How to use it

  1. List the bodies you want, separated by commas.
  2. Read the first body as the reference for the ratios.
  3. Look at the picture and the table under it.
  4. Change the order to use another reference body.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the data from?

The NASA Planetary Fact Sheet; the page prints the source.

Are distances to scale?

No, only the sizes.

Why is Pluto included?

It is on the fact sheet and is useful as a small comparison.

Are the numbers rounded?

They are as published, for example 12,756 km for Earth.
