BMR & TDEE Calculator

Enter your sex, age, weight, height and activity level. The tool estimates your basal metabolic rate and maintenance calories, with simple calorie targets for loss or gain.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

If you want to eat to lose, maintain or gain weight, you need a starting figure for how many calories your body burns in a day. Your basal metabolic rate is what you burn at rest; multiply by an activity factor and you get total daily energy expenditure. This page uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a widely used estimate, and lists the five usual activity levels with their multipliers so you can choose honestly. It then shows a mild reduction, a 500 kcal deficit and a 500 kcal surplus as reference points. These are estimates and individual metabolism varies, so adjust after a few weeks of real results. The numbers never leave your device and this is not medical advice.

How to use it

  1. Choose your sex and enter your age, weight in kilograms and height in centimetres.
  2. Select the activity level that best matches a typical week.
  3. Read your BMR, maintenance calories and the loss and gain targets.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy your body uses at complete rest. TDEE is BMR multiplied by an activity factor, covering movement and exercise, and is the maintenance level for weight stability.

How accurate is the estimate?

Mifflin-St Jeor is reasonable for many adults but can be off by several hundred calories for individuals. Treat it as a starting point and adjust from your own results.

Does the tool accept pounds or inches?

No. Weight must be in kilograms and height in centimetres. Convert first, or use the unit converter on this site.

Is a 500 kcal deficit always safe?

Not necessarily. It is a common reference point and not a recommendation. Pregnancy, medical conditions, age and very low intakes all need personal advice from a health professional.
