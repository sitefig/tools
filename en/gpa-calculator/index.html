GPA Calculator

Enter one course per line in the form 'name grade credits', such as 'Calculus A 4'. The tool returns total credits, quality points and a credit-weighted GPA.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Every term you end up with a list of courses, letter grades and credit values, and you want one figure. Typing them into a form each time is slow, and a mistake with credit weighting is easy to make: a B in a four-credit lab counts for more than an A in a one-credit seminar. This calculator takes the list as plain text, so you can paste it from notes or a spreadsheet and edit it freely. It understands A+ to F including plus and minus grades, or a number you supply directly, and reports credits, quality points and the final GPA to two decimals. It uses a 4.0 scale without extra weight for honours courses. Your grades stay in your browser.

How to use it

  1. Write one course per line: the course name, then the grade, then the credits.
  2. Use letter grades such as A-, B+ or C, or a number of points.
  3. Read the total credits, quality points and your GPA.

Frequently asked questions

Which grade scale is used?

A and A+ are 4.0, A- is 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B- 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C- 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, D- 0.7 and F 0. Many universities differ, so check yours.

What does a bad line error mean?

Each line must end with the credits and have a grade just before it. A missing credit value, an unknown grade or a credit of zero stops the calculation and names the line.

Can I include honours or AP weighting?

Not automatically. You can enter a number such as 5 as the grade for a weighted course instead of a letter, and it will be used as the grade points.

Is this an official GPA?

No. It is an unofficial calculation for your own planning. Your institution's registrar or student records office sets the official scale and rounding rules.
