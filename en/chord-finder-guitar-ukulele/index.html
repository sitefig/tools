Guitar and Ukulele Chord Finder with Fingering Diagrams

Looks up the notes of a chord and searches the fretboard for playable fingerings, then draws them as chord diagrams.

Use the tool

Why this tool exists

Chord charts in books show one shape per chord. Here you type any root and one of sixteen chord types and the tool finds several fingerings: it searches a four-fret window for shapes that contain every chord tone, start on the root (on guitar) and need at most four fingers, counting a barre as one.

The shapes are suggestions that are guaranteed to contain the right notes, which was checked for 1300 generated fingerings across all roots and types. They are not the only or the traditional shapes, and ranking by ease is a heuristic. Sound is not played on this page.

How to use it

  1. Type chord names separated by commas, such as C, Am7, F#m.
  2. Choose guitar or ukulele.
  3. Read the diagrams: x is muted, a circle is an open string.
  4. Read the fret numbers and notes in the text list.

Frequently asked questions

Which chord types work?

Major, m, 7, maj7, m7, dim, dim7, m7b5, aug, sus2, sus4, 6, m6, 9, add9 and 5.

Why are some chords missing on ukulele?

Four strings cannot hold every tone of a five-note chord or a power chord.

Are the shapes standard?

They are correct but may differ from what a book shows.

Can I hear the chord?

Not here. Try the virtual piano or an instrument tuner.
